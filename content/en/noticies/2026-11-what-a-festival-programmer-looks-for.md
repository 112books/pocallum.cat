I have been sending photos to festival programmers for over fifteen years, and I have learned that what makes them decide has nothing to do with what photographers care about. They don't look at composition. They look at whether the image helps them fill a room.

This is what they actually look for:

**1. That you can be recognised.** If the poster carries a name, the photo must make it obvious who it is. A shot that is too wide, or a silhouette, won't do: whoever reads the programme has to think "that's them" in two seconds.

**2. That the stage is visible.** Programmers sell an experience, not a portrait. They want audience, lights, atmosphere. A photo of the musician alone against black is a portfolio shot, not a sales tool.

**3. That it works horizontally… and vertically.** They need the same show in 16:9 for the website, 1:1 for Instagram and vertical for the printed poster. If you only have one, they will ask for the one you're missing.

**4. That you can see there were people.** This is the most underrated one. A full room is the strongest sales argument there is.

**5. That it doesn't smell of press.** No giant watermarks, and no festival logo burned in unless you are the official photographer. If the image is yours, you should be able to prove it without ruining it.

**6. That it arrives on time.** Not the day before the festival: the day they close the line-up, often months earlier. A well-organised archive and being findable is half the job.

If you want to cover festivals, have these six things ready before you knock on the door. And if you want us to handle them for you, you know where we are.
